## Selection protocol

Choose structure before surface styling.

1. Identify the audience's main intent: learn, evaluate, buy, book, visit,
   participate, browse, publish, or operate.
2. Identify the dominant content unit: story, work, product, service, place,
   event, lesson, record, or live data.
3. Choose one primary archetype below. Add at most one secondary influence.
4. Choose a composition model, density, navigation model, media role, and proof
   format from the dimensions section.
5. Sketch the page as named content blocks before choosing colors or effects.

Do not choose SaaS/product marketing unless software evaluation or adoption is
the actual user intent. Do not force every site into a conversion funnel.

## Archetype library

### Editorial or media

- **Job:** help readers discover, understand, and continue reading.
- **Content units:** stories, issues, authors, topics, dates.
- **Good structures:** front page, news desk, magazine index, long-form feature,
  chronological stream.
- **Distinctive moves:** typographic hierarchy, asymmetric story weighting,
  visible metadata, related-story trails.
- **Avoid:** feature-card grids that make every story look equally important.

### Portfolio or studio

- **Job:** make the work and point of view memorable enough to earn contact.
- **Content units:** projects, disciplines, outcomes, process, credits.
- **Good structures:** project reel, case-study index, art-book sequence,
  annotated archive.
- **Distinctive moves:** work-first opening, variable project scale, strong
  captions, case-study transitions.
- **Avoid:** a generic agency hero followed by three service cards.

### Commerce or catalog

- **Job:** support confident discovery, comparison, and purchase.
- **Content units:** products, collections, variants, price, availability.
- **Good structures:** collection wall, guided assortment, lookbook, editorial
  catalog, utility-first product finder.
- **Distinctive moves:** persistent filters, material detail, comparison,
  merchandising rhythm, trust near the buying action.
- **Avoid:** marketing sections that delay access to products.

### Local service or professional practice

- **Job:** establish relevance and trust, then enable contact or booking.
- **Content units:** services, location, availability, credentials, cases.
- **Good structures:** service menu, appointment path, neighborhood guide,
  evidence-led profile.
- **Distinctive moves:** concrete locality, visible hours and response times,
  process clarity, human credentials.
- **Avoid:** invented scale, vague global claims, and fake metrics.

### Hospitality, travel, or place

- **Job:** convey atmosphere while making planning and booking easy.
- **Content units:** rooms or spaces, amenities, location, dates, experiences.
- **Good structures:** cinematic place story, practical trip planner, room
  index, seasonal guide.
- **Distinctive moves:** immersive but purposeful media, map context, date and
  availability controls, sensory copy.
- **Avoid:** hiding essential booking facts behind mood imagery.

### Restaurant, café, or bar

- **Job:** make people hungry or curious, then answer "can I go, when, and
  how" in seconds.
- **Content units:** menu, dishes, hours, address, booking, the room, the
  people cooking.
- **Good structures:** menu-first page, tonight/today board, reservation path,
  seasonal menu story.
- **Distinctive moves:** menu typography with real prices, today's
  availability, hours and address visible without scrolling on mobile, food
  photography with appetite.
- **Avoid:** atmosphere that hides hours, address, or booking; menus as PDFs or
  images of text.

### Culture, event, or entertainment

- **Job:** create anticipation and communicate what, where, and when.
- **Content units:** program, performers, venue, schedule, tickets.
- **Good structures:** poster-led announcement, timetable, lineup atlas,
  program booklet.
- **Distinctive moves:** expressive typography, temporal rhythm, schedule
  clarity, venue orientation.
- **Avoid:** using SaaS testimonial and feature sections for cultural proof.

### Nonprofit, civic, or community

- **Job:** explain the cause, demonstrate impact, and enable participation.
- **Content units:** mission, programs, people, evidence, actions, updates.
- **Good structures:** issue explainer, impact report, campaign hub, community
  bulletin.
- **Distinctive moves:** transparent evidence, human stories, multiple action
  paths, accessible language.
- **Avoid:** emotional pressure without substantiation or financial clarity.

### Education or course

- **Job:** help learners judge fit and understand the learning path.
- **Content units:** outcomes, syllabus, lessons, instructors, prerequisites.
- **Good structures:** syllabus spine, cohort guide, learning map, resource
  library.
- **Distinctive moves:** progression, sample material, time commitment,
  instructor evidence.
- **Avoid:** outcome promises without curriculum or assessment detail.

### Documentation or knowledge base

- **Job:** get users to a correct answer quickly and preserve context.
- **Content units:** concepts, tasks, APIs, examples, versions.
- **Good structures:** task-first docs, reference manual, concept graph,
  searchable handbook.
- **Distinctive moves:** strong navigation, readable measures, code and prose
  hierarchy, version visibility.
- **Avoid:** marketing ornament inside the reading path.

### Directory, marketplace, or listings

- **Job:** help users search, filter, compare, and assess many records.
- **Content units:** listings, attributes, location, availability, reputation.
- **Good structures:** map and list, faceted directory, ranked index, category
  explorer.
- **Distinctive moves:** useful result density, query persistence, comparison,
  transparent sorting.
- **Avoid:** oversized hero content that pushes results below the fold.

### Personal site, blog, or creator

- **Job:** express a specific person's voice and work, and make it easy to
  read, follow, hire, or support them.
- **Content units:** posts, projects, talks, newsletter, about, contact.
- **Good structures:** writing index, single-column journal, digital garden,
  link-rich home page, newsletter archive.
- **Distinctive moves:** the person's own voice in microcopy, idiosyncratic
  but readable typography, dates and tags, RSS.
- **Avoid:** a personal brand dressed as a startup landing page.

### Single product or brand launch

- **Job:** make one product desirable and understandable, then sell or
  capture interest.
- **Content units:** the product, its materials or ingredients, variants,
  price, how it is used, availability.
- **Good structures:** product story in scenes, ingredient or material
  breakdown, configurator, pre-order page.
- **Distinctive moves:** product scale and detail, sensory specifics, honest
  availability, purchase path always one tap away.
- **Avoid:** generic lifestyle filler and SaaS feature grids for a physical
  product.

### Corporate, B2B services, or industrial

- **Job:** show capability and reliability to buyers who compare suppliers
  slowly and in committees.
- **Content units:** capabilities, sectors, projects, facilities,
  certifications, people, locations.
- **Good structures:** capability index, project archive with specifics,
  sector entry paths, facility tour, request-for-quote path.
- **Distinctive moves:** named projects with numbers and constraints, real
  facilities and equipment, downloadable specifications, direct contacts.
- **Avoid:** stock handshake photography, "solutions" without nouns, and
  invented client logos.

### Product or SaaS marketing

- **Job:** explain a product, prove fit, and start adoption or sales.
- **Content units:** use cases, capabilities, workflow, integrations, proof.
- **Good structures:** problem-to-workflow narrative, interactive product tour,
  role-based entry, technical evaluation path.
- **Distinctive moves:** real product evidence, credible constraints, clear
  trial or sales path.
- **Avoid:** treating this archetype as the universal website template.

### Dashboard or operational application

- **Job:** help a user monitor, decide, and act repeatedly.
- **Content units:** records, status, alerts, trends, actions.
- **Good structures:** overview and drill-down, work queue, command center,
  canvas with contextual tools.
- **Distinctive moves:** state hierarchy, progressive disclosure, dense but
  calm controls, designed failure and empty states.
- **Avoid:** landing-page composition inside authenticated workflows.

## Composition dimensions

Select deliberately; do not always choose the first option.

- **Page silhouette:** editorial stack, split narrative, index wall, continuous
  canvas, chronological stream, map-led explorer, poster, catalog, or utility
  workspace.
- **Density:** spacious showcase, balanced reading, compact index, or dense
  operational view.
- **Navigation:** minimal top level, section index, persistent sidebar, search
  first, category rail, or task-based navigation.
- **Hierarchy:** one dominant story, ranked clusters, repeated records,
  stepwise progression, or overview with drill-down.
- **Media role:** proof, instruction, atmosphere, orientation, or none.
- **Proof format:** case evidence, sourced statistics, curriculum, credentials,
  product demonstration, transparent process, reviews, or public records.
- **Primary action:** continue reading, compare, purchase, book, contact,
  register, donate, contribute, search, or operate.

Vary at least three of these dimensions when moving between unrelated projects.
Color and font changes alone do not constitute a new direction.

The archetype fixes structure; expression comes next, from the source world and
the direction families in [aesthetic-directions.md](aesthetic-directions.md).
The same archetype can and should look very different across subjects: two
restaurants may share a menu-first structure while one reads as a candle-lit
printed card and the other as a two-ink market flyer.

## Hybrid sites

Use a primary archetype for the information architecture and a secondary
influence for expression. Examples:

- a hotel site with an editorial travel-guide influence;
- a portfolio presented as an archival catalog;
- a nonprofit annual report with a data-journalism influence;
- an ecommerce site with a restrained lookbook influence;
- technical documentation with a learning-path influence.

Do not blend more than two archetypes on one route. When user intents conflict,
separate them into routes instead of making one page serve every purpose.

## Diversity checks

Before implementation, compare the proposed structure with the last relevant
site or the repository's existing templates when available.

1. Is the hero geometry meaningfully different, or is there a hero at all?
2. Is the dominant content unit different?
3. Does navigation match this user's intent?
4. Does the section order follow the content rather than a stock funnel?
5. Are proof and calls to action native to this archetype?
6. Would swapping in unrelated SaaS copy leave the layout mostly intact?

If the answer to question 6 is yes, revise the structure before styling.
