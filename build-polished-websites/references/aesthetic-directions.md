## How to use this library

These families widen the range of candidates; they are not templates. For each
candidate in [visual-direction.md](visual-direction.md):

1. Pick the family whose *fits* line matches the source world, not the one that
   looked best last time.
2. Change at least two of its starting values (typeface, palette, silhouette,
   signature) to values drawn from the source world.
3. Combine at most two families: one primary, one influence.
4. Name the family and every changed value in the direction card.

Palettes below are starting points expressed as roles. Sample real values from
the subject (photos, packaging, signage, the brand) whenever possible, then
check contrast. Font names are Google Fonts families unless marked as system
stacks; production builds should self-host the subsets they use.

## Twenty direction families

### 1. Swiss modernist

- **Fits:** architecture, design schools, transit, conferences, museums,
  engineering firms, rigorous B2B.
- **Type:** one neo-grotesk for everything: Schibsted Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk,
  Archivo, or a Helvetica/Arial system stack. Two weights. Flush left, ragged
  right. Large numerals.
- **Palette:** white ground, near-black ink, one pure signal colour (a true
  red or ultramarine) reserved for one function.
- **Layout:** visible modular grid; strong left axis; text blocks hang from a
  shared top line; scale alone ranks information.
- **Signature:** a typographic poster opening where size, position, and one
  rule do all the work.
- **Motion and imagery:** near-instant state changes; black-and-white
  photography or diagrams.
- **Pitfall:** without real content it reads as empty, not restrained.

### 2. Magazine editorial

- **Fits:** media, long-form, culture brands, publishers, journals, thoughtful
  personal sites.
- **Type:** display serif with real italics (Newsreader at large optical size,
  Libre Caslon Display, Gloock) and a text serif (Source Serif 4, Literata) or
  a quiet sans for metadata.
- **Palette:** clean white or very light neutral ground (not cream by reflex),
  black ink, one spot colour per issue or section.
- **Layout:** asymmetric story weighting, bylines and dates visible, pull
  quotes, multi-column text on wide screens, real captions.
- **Signature:** a front page where lead-story size reflects importance; drop
  caps or section flags derived from the publication's name.
- **Motion and imagery:** reading-first, almost no motion; photography with
  captions and credits.
- **Pitfall:** treating every story as an equal card.

### 3. Luxury minimal

- **Fits:** fashion, jewellery, perfume, high-end hotels, galleries, premium
  real estate.
- **Type:** high-contrast didone display (Bodoni Moda, Italiana, Gloock; Prata
  for Cyrillic) with a widely spaced small sans (Jost, Tenor Sans).
- **Palette:** near-white or near-black; no accent, or one muted material tone
  (bronze, oxblood, slate) used sparingly.
- **Layout:** large single images, wide margins, small precise text, sparse
  navigation, generous vertical pacing.
- **Signature:** slow cross-fade or crop reveal of one product or space.
- **Motion and imagery:** slow, long easing; photography is mandatory.
- **Pitfall:** collapses without strong imagery; plan imagery first.

### 4. Heritage institution

- **Fits:** law firms, banks, universities, foundations, archives, old family
  businesses.
- **Type:** classic book serif (Libre Caslon Text, EB Garamond, Crimson Pro)
  with a restrained sans for interface (Public Sans, IBM Plex Sans).
- **Palette:** white or stone ground with deep ink, navy, oxblood, or forest
  green; metallic tone only for the mark.
- **Layout:** structured columns, ledger rules, numbered clauses, clear
  practice or department index, visible people and credentials.
- **Signature:** a custom SVG monogram or seal built from the founders'
  initials or the institution's date.
- **Motion and imagery:** minimal; portraits and buildings photographed
  plainly.
- **Pitfall:** stiffness. Keep language plain and actions prominent.

### 5. Calm care

- **Fits:** clinics, therapy, family services, pharmacies, elder care,
  sensitive legal matters, public services.
- **Type:** highly legible humanist sans: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Figtree,
  Source Sans 3, Lexend. Larger base size (18–20 px).
- **Palette:** soft tinted neutrals (mist blue, sage, warm stone) with one
  clear, accessible action colour; never clinical pure white plus cyan.
- **Layout:** step-by-step paths, short sections, plain-language headings,
  large tap targets, reassurance next to every action.
- **Signature:** a "what happens next" sequence that removes uncertainty about
  the first visit or call.
- **Motion and imagery:** gentle, minimal; real people and places, or warm
  simple illustration.
- **Pitfall:** blandness. Specific facts and human detail create warmth.

### 6. Colour-field brand

- **Fits:** consumer apps, direct-to-consumer products, drinks, food brands,
  youth-oriented services.
- **Type:** characterful display grotesk (Bricolage Grotesque, Familjen
  Grotesk, Gabarito, Unbounded) with a simple text sans.
- **Palette:** two to four saturated flat brand colours used as full-bleed
  section grounds, each with its own text colour pair.
- **Layout:** big colour blocks, oversized product, confident scale jumps,
  stickers or badges only if the packaging has them.
- **Signature:** the whole palette shifts as the user scrolls between flavours,
  products, or moods.
- **Motion and imagery:** springy, playful micro-interactions; cut-out product
  photography or bold illustration.
- **Pitfall:** pastel sameness. Commit to the brand's real colours.

### 7. Dark cinematic product

- **Fits:** hardware, cameras, cars, audio, premium devices, games.
- **Type:** precise modern sans (Geist, Red Hat Display, Archivo with width
  axis) set large with tight but not crushed tracking.
- **Palette:** near-black ground (not `#000`), product-lit highlights sampled
  from the product's own materials, never a default purple glow.
- **Layout:** one product idea per viewport, sticky scroll scenes, spec tables
  that read like data sheets.
- **Signature:** a scroll-linked reveal of the product's defining detail.
- **Motion and imagery:** cinematic but short; renders or studio photography
  are essential.
- **Pitfall:** without product imagery it becomes an empty dark page.

### 8. Precision software

- **Fits:** B2B SaaS, developer tools, fintech, infrastructure, analytics.
- **Type:** neutral sans with tabular numerals (Geist, IBM Plex Sans, Hanken
  Grotesk) plus a mono used only for code and data.
- **Palette:** neutral tinted toward the brand hue, one brand colour, honest
  status colours; light and dark themes.
- **Layout:** real product UI rebuilt in HTML with real-looking data, 1 px
  borders, dense but calm information, workflow-led narrative.
- **Signature:** an interactive slice of the product the visitor can operate.
- **Motion and imagery:** quick, functional transitions; the product is the
  image.
- **Pitfall:** sliding back into the classic gradient template. Show the work.

### 9. Technical drawing

- **Fits:** engineering, manufacturing, architecture practices, hardware,
  research labs.
- **Type:** condensed technical sans (IBM Plex Sans Condensed, Chivo, B612)
  with annotation-sized labels.
- **Palette:** graphite on white, or white lines on deep blueprint blue; one
  highlight for callouts.
- **Layout:** dimension lines, numbered callouts, exploded diagrams, section
  views, specification tables.
- **Signature:** an SVG drawing whose callouts link to the content sections.
- **Motion and imagery:** line drawing animations on entry only; SVG diagrams.
- **Pitfall:** decoration-only grids. Every line should measure something real.

### 10. Data report

- **Fits:** nonprofits, research, civic projects, annual and impact reports,
  public data.
- **Type:** text serif (Source Serif 4, Newsreader) with a chart-friendly sans
  (Libre Franklin, Public Sans) and tabular numerals.
- **Palette:** white ground, dark ink, a sequential or categorical data palette
  validated for colour-blind readers.
- **Layout:** scrollytelling, charts as heroes, annotated numbers, footnotes
  and sources inline.
- **Signature:** the key finding as an interactive or annotated chart.
- **Motion and imagery:** data transitions that preserve object constancy;
  charts, maps, documentary photography.
- **Pitfall:** numbers without sources.

### 11. Kinetic poster

- **Fits:** festivals, concerts, sports clubs, conferences, launches,
  exhibitions.
- **Type:** variable display faces with wide axes (Anybody, Big Shoulders
  Display, Archivo width axis; Unbounded or Geologica for Cyrillic) at extreme
  sizes.
- **Palette:** two or three high-contrast colours taken from the event's
  poster, venue, or team; full-bleed.
- **Layout:** type as the image; date, place, and lineup as the composition;
  schedule as a typographic grid.
- **Signature:** variable-axis or scale animation tied to scroll or time,
  paused under reduced motion.
- **Motion and imagery:** energetic but controllable; posters, performers,
  venue.
- **Pitfall:** sacrificing schedule and ticket clarity for spectacle.

### 12. Zine collage

- **Fits:** youth culture, streetwear, independent music, art events,
  community radio.
- **Type:** up to three faces with explicit roles: a heavy display (Archivo
  Black, Rubik Mono One), a serif (Old Standard TT), a typewriter (Courier
  Prime).
- **Palette:** clashing but limited: photocopy black, one fluorescent, one
  paper tone.
- **Layout:** overlapping cut-outs, rotated elements, broken grid, hand-placed
  stickers, while body text stays calm and readable.
- **Signature:** a cut-and-paste hero assembled from the subject's own
  ephemera.
- **Motion and imagery:** jittery stop-motion; scanned or photocopied imagery.
- **Pitfall:** illegibility and inaccessible contrast.

### 13. Two-ink print

- **Fits:** bookshops, small publishers, bakeries, community groups, craft
  fairs, cafés.
- **Type:** characterful slab or casual (Recursive with its casual axis, Zilla
  Slab, Alfa Slab One for display only).
- **Palette:** two spot inks on white stock (for example fluorescent pink with
  medium blue, or yellow with green), overprinted with `mix-blend-mode:
  multiply`.
- **Layout:** halftone imagery, slight misregistration, print marks, flyer
  rhythm.
- **Signature:** illustrations that separate into the two ink layers on hover
  or scroll.
- **Motion and imagery:** minimal; halftoned photos and two-colour drawings.
- **Pitfall:** turning it into generic beige "craft". Keep the ink colours
  bright and the stock white.

### 14. Wayfinding and transit

- **Fits:** venues, campuses, airports, tourism, logistics, festivals spread
  over a site, parks.
- **Type:** signage sans (Overpass, Public Sans, Red Hat Text) with bold
  pictograms and arrows.
- **Palette:** high-visibility signage colours or transit line colours with
  strict meaning.
- **Layout:** route lines as structure, stations as steps, maps and
  directional cues, zone colour-coding.
- **Signature:** a schematic map that is also the navigation.
- **Motion and imagery:** route tracing, live status; maps, pictograms.
- **Pitfall:** colour codes that mean nothing.

### 15. Quiet craft

- **Fits:** furniture, tea, ceramics, Japanese or Nordic design goods,
  architecture studios, small hotels.
- **Type:** light humanist or gothic sans (Zen Kaku Gothic New, Hanken
  Grotesk), optional mincho or book serif (Shippori Mincho), small sizes,
  possibly vertical text accents with `writing-mode`.
- **Palette:** sampled from a named material (oiled oak, slate, washi,
  indigo-dyed cloth). This family sits closest to the cream default, so the
  card must name the material the ground colour came from.
- **Layout:** asymmetric emptiness, small type, large single images, slow
  reading order.
- **Signature:** an object rotating or changing light across the day.
- **Motion and imagery:** slow fades; material close-ups.
- **Pitfall:** generic beige minimalism.

### 16. Organic place

- **Fits:** outdoor brands, farms, vineyards, parks, retreats, regional
  tourism.
- **Type:** soft serif (Young Serif, Literata, Alegreya) with a humanist sans
  (Alegreya Sans).
- **Palette:** sampled from the specific landscape: lichen, granite, peat,
  heather, sea; seasonal shifts allowed.
- **Layout:** layered imagery, seasonal calendar, trail or map structure.
- **Signature:** the page palette or hero changes with the current season or
  time of day.
- **Motion and imagery:** gentle parallax at most; landscape photography,
  botanical drawing, maps. Use contour lines only for real terrain.
- **Pitfall:** stock-nature clichés (leaf icons, green gradients).

### 17. Playful illustrated

- **Fits:** children, learning products, pets, friendly consumer apps,
  community events.
- **Type:** rounded friendly faces (Fredoka, Baloo 2, Lexend; Rubik for
  Cyrillic) at generous sizes.
- **Palette:** bright, balanced primaries with a deep ink for text; avoid
  candy pastels unless the brand uses them.
- **Layout:** illustrated scenes, characters, wavy or hand-drawn dividers,
  oversized buttons.
- **Signature:** a mascot or scene that reacts to interaction.
- **Motion and imagery:** bouncy, quick, interruptible; consistent SVG
  illustration system.
- **Pitfall:** childish tone when parents or teachers are the audience.

### 18. Retro computing

- **Fits:** indie software, developer events, games, hacker spaces,
  open-source projects.
- **Type:** pixel or terminal accents (Silkscreen, Pixelify Sans, VT323) with a
  legible text face for paragraphs.
- **Palette:** system greys with bevels, or phosphor on black, or a specific
  machine's palette.
- **Layout:** windows, menus, dialogs, desktop metaphor, file listings.
- **Signature:** a usable mini "OS" interaction (draggable window, menu bar).
- **Motion and imagery:** stepped, frame-based; pixel art, screenshots.
- **Pitfall:** gimmick over usability; keep keyboard and screen-reader paths
  intact.

### 19. Raw web

- **Fits:** artists, writers, experimental studios, personal sites, small
  archives.
- **Type:** system serif (`Times New Roman`, `Georgia`) and `ui-monospace`,
  default sizes deliberately chosen.
- **Palette:** browser defaults: white, black, link blue `#0000ee`, visited
  purple `#551a8b`, or one exception.
- **Layout:** plain HTML flow, tables, rules, lists, visible structure.
- **Signature:** content density and honesty; one surprising interaction.
- **Motion and imagery:** none or abrupt; images at native size.
- **Pitfall:** confusing it with neo-brutalism (pastel cards plus offset
  shadows), which is now a template of its own.

### 20. Night hospitality

- **Fits:** cocktail bars, restaurants, clubs, theatres, jazz venues, hotels at
  night.
- **Type:** deco or display serif (Marcellus, Bodoni Moda, Limelight for very
  short words only) with a clean sans for menus and practical details.
- **Palette:** deep bottle green, oxblood, or ink with brass and candle-warm
  highlights; low-key contrast tuned for legibility.
- **Layout:** menu typography with dot leaders between item and price,
  programme listings, reservation path always visible.
- **Signature:** the menu or programme as a beautifully set printed card.
- **Motion and imagery:** slow light-like transitions; low-light interior and
  food photography.
- **Pitfall:** mood that hides opening hours, address, and booking.

## Genre worlds

A genre world turns the whole site into a place with its own rules: a pixel
game level, an isometric town, a paper diorama. It suits games, toys, kids'
products, creative studios, events, and playful brands, and works best as the
expression layer over a normal, accessible document. The world frames the
content; it never replaces readable HTML text, real links, and real buttons.

Rules for every genre world:

- Text, navigation, and forms stay in HTML above or inside the world, with
  normal contrast and focus.
- Decorative canvases and scenes get `aria-hidden="true"`; anything meaningful
  in them is repeated in text.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the world becomes a still scene.
- Pause animation when the tab is hidden or the scene is off-screen
  (`IntersectionObserver`, `visibilitychange`), and cap frame rate.
- Budget it: a world that pushes LCP past the gate or TBT past 200 ms is cut
  down, not exempted.

### Pixel world

- **Build:** draw sprites and tiles at native size (16 or 32 px) as PNG, SVG
  `<rect>` grids, or canvas, and scale only by whole numbers with
  `image-rendering: pixelated`. Snap positions to the pixel grid.
- **Palette:** a fixed 8–32 colour palette (a classic console palette or one
  derived from the brand), no anti-aliased gradients.
- **Layers:** 3–5 parallax layers (sky, far hills, near scenery, ground) moved
  by scroll or time in whole-pixel steps; a tiled ground strip that loops.
- **Type:** pixel face (Silkscreen, Pixelify Sans, Press Start 2P, VT323) for
  headings and UI chrome only; a legible text face for paragraphs.
- **Motion:** `steps()` easing, sprite-sheet animation via
  `background-position`, 8–12 fps character cycles.
- **Signature:** a character or object that walks along as the user scrolls,
  or sections styled as levels with the primary action as the goal flag.

### Isometric world

- **Build:** SVG on a 2:1 isometric grid (30° angles), or CSS
  `transform: rotateX(60deg) rotateZ(-45deg)` on a flat plan; consistent light
  from one side with three face tones per block.
- **Fits:** logistics, cities, venues, games, product ecosystems, maps of how
  something works.
- **Signature:** an isometric map whose buildings are the navigation.

### Paper and cut-out

- **Build:** layered SVG or images with irregular cut edges (SVG masks), soft
  contact shadows, and subtle parallax between layers; real paper scans when
  available.
- **Fits:** children's education, stationery, crafts, theatre, storytelling.
- **Signature:** a diorama hero whose layers separate on scroll.

### Hand-drawn sketch

- **Build:** SVG strokes with slight irregularity (a light `feTurbulence` +
  `feDisplacementMap` on lines only), notebook or blueprint grounds,
  marker highlights on key words.
- **Fits:** studios, education, early-stage products, workshops.
- **Signature:** diagrams that draw themselves once, with
  `stroke-dashoffset`.

### 3D and WebGL scene

- **Build:** three.js or a similar library loaded only on the route that needs
  it, a static poster image first, the scene initialised after load and only on
  capable devices; models compressed (glTF + Draco/Meshopt, KTX2 textures).
- **Fits:** products whose form matters, games, architecture, immersive
  events.
- **Signature:** the product or space the user can rotate or walk through.

### Y2K and chrome

- **Build:** chrome and holographic gradients built deliberately from a small
  set of stops, bevelled UI, star and sparkle glyphs used as a period
  vocabulary, extended or bubbly display faces.
- **Fits:** music, nightlife, fashion, youth brands with that heritage.
- **Pitfall:** the same gradients and sparkles are first-generation template
  tells everywhere else; use them only when the era is the point.

## Type and script notes

- Confirm that every chosen family covers every language on the site.
  Families with reliable Cyrillic coverage include Golos Text, Onest,
  Geologica, Unbounded, Jost, Rubik, Manrope, IBM Plex Sans and Mono,
  Literata, Source Serif 4, Source Sans 3, Spectral, PT Serif, PT Sans,
  Alegreya, EB Garamond, Cormorant, Prata, and Tenor Sans. Check the subset
  list on the font page before committing.
- One display and one text family is the default. A third family needs a
  stated role (for example code or data).
- Variable fonts let one file carry weight, width, and optical size. Prefer
  them when the direction uses several weights or widths.
- System stacks are a legitimate choice for the raw-web and some Swiss
  directions, or when performance budgets forbid web fonts. Choose them
  deliberately and say so in the card.
